This week, we mostly focused on finishing our open-source projects. We, in particular, were recommended to present on the first day, and we decided to get started on our presentation. After a month of work and development, this was a big moment.

<!--more--> 

I'd like to reflect briefly on how rewarding of an experience this was. I was the one who suggested nanobot for our team's project, and I'm really glad we chose it. The community is so active and vibrant, and the project is genuinely cool. Every single one of us is a user now. 
I had to learn a _lot_ about agentic AI workflows, how to make them efficient, how to manage context. It was a lot of reading, and ultimately super rewarding.
I also think these are going to be vital skills for me going forward. I'm so proud to be able to say that _I_ added features for the tools that I use. 

I did have a minor experience that put a bad taste in my mouth this week in nanobot, though. An issue was opened to fix a MCP normalisation bug, and it was marked as a good first issue. I saw the issue within an _hour_ of it being posted, and got to work immediately. 
When I pushed, however, my PR was rejected because one of the maintainers had fixed it just 30 minutes ago. It was a frustrating waste of effort, but I did still learn quite a bit, and I'm ultimately glad the problem was solved. 
I just wish the maintainer had left it open for a bit longer - nanobot isn't exactly a dead project and it would have been better left for a new contributor.
