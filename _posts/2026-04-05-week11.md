This post will pick up on my thoughts from last week's post, because I accidentally discussed quite a bit of "The Cathedral and the Bazaar" in last week's post. 

<!--more-->
As I mentioned last week, my largest takeaway from the reading was that community was one of the largest factors in the success of an open-source project. I do, however, wonder how this compares to the modern-day open-source software world, where many of the largest bazaars are essentially subsidised by some of the largest cathedrals. Microsoft, for example, was a frequent target of Raymond's in this reading, but in 2026, it funds so much open-source development, _owns_ the platform that almost all these "bazaars" are distributing their code (Github) and open-sources much of their centrally designed code as well. Raymond discusses that the bazaar is the most effective way to build open-source software, but it seems to rely on the cathedral to survive.

I'm obviously not saying that open-source software _could not_ survive on its own; it could and it has in the past. But I do think that the frequent villainization of corporate development in the open-source community is short-sighted.
